# The AI content team, seven employees on Claude Code

An outside blueprint Byron saved on 15 August 2026. A ten slide carousel by
`ibraviz.ai` describing a content operation run by one person and seven named
AI employees, all built on Claude Code. It is worth keeping because it is the
same idea as [[C-Suite]] pointed at a different job: one named role per stage,
each with a stated deliverable, and a human who only approves.

It is also an advert. The last slide sells the setup, so read the numbers as
claims rather than as findings.

## The shape of it

One creator, one office, seven employees. Seven stages, in order:

`Research` -> `Hook` -> `Script` -> `Design` -> `Analyze` -> `Plan` -> `Publish`

and then it repeats. Each stage has exactly one employee who owns it:

| Stage | Employee |
| --- | --- |
| Research | The Researcher |
| Hook | The Hook Writer |
| Script | The Script Writer |
| Design | The Designer |
| Analyze | The Analyst |
| Plan | The Manager |
| Publish | The Publisher |

The line under the loop is the whole pitch: **your only job is approving the
output.** The human is a gate on the way out, not a worker inside it.

## The three employees the post described in full

**The Researcher.** "It finds every viral opportunity." It tracks competitors
by watching top creators and brands, finds trends by spotting patterns before
they go viral, saves high performing posts as references, and looks for
untapped niches. Its toolkit is Reels, TikTok, Shorts, X and Reddit, claimed as
forty sources scanned. It delivers trend reports, content ideas, viral
references and opportunity lists. Its method is three steps: discover, collect,
report.

**The Designer.** "It designs every post." Figma for layouts and carousels,
Canva for quick graphics and thumbnails, AI images to match any idea,
Photoshop for editing and mockups. It works to a fixed brand, an ink blue
accent and SF Pro. It delivers carousel designs, thumbnails chosen for click
through, reel covers, templates, mockups and brand assets. Its method is five
steps: brief, concept, design, revise, deliver.

**The Manager.** "It runs the whole company." It plans what gets created and
when, assigns work to the other six and keeps it moving, connects the systems
so the handoffs happen without being asked, tracks every employee's output, and
publishes daily. What it delivers is not content at all: everything connected,
everything automated, one source of truth.

The slides for the Hook Writer, the Script Writer, the Analyst and the
Publisher did not come back readable when this was saved. Their names and their
place in the loop are certain; their toolkits and their methods are not
recorded here, and nothing has been guessed in their place.

## What is worth taking

Three things, and they are all about shape rather than about content.

**Every role states what it delivers.** Not what it works on, what lands on the
desk at the end. "Trend reports, content ideas, viral references, opportunity
lists" is a contract you can hold a desk to. "Monitors the market" is not. Our
seven desks each have levers and a `learn()` pass, and most of them can say
what they changed, but not all of them can say what they produced.

**Every role states its method as steps.** Discover, collect, report. Brief,
concept, design, revise, deliver. Three or five steps, always in order, always
the same ones. That is what makes an employee's output judgeable by somebody
who did not watch it work.

**One role owns the routing and is the single source of truth.** Their Manager
is our [[CEO]] and `comms`: the one seat that decides whose job a thing is,
keeps the handoffs moving, and is the only place you go to find out where
anything got to. Their version publishes as well, which ours deliberately does
not, because nothing in our machine may send by itself.

The stage it has that we do not is **Analyze between making and planning.** In
their loop the analyst sits after design and before the manager plans the next
round, so the next batch is chosen by what the last batch did. Our desks learn
from outcomes one number at a time, in [[Memory]] under Lessons, which is the
same instinct arriving one lever at a time rather than as a stage of the loop.
