# The AI content team, seven employees on Claude Code

An outside blueprint Byron saved on 15 August 2026. A ten slide carousel by
`ibraviz.ai` describing a content operation run by one person and seven named AI
employees, all built on Claude Code. It is worth keeping because it is the same
idea as [[C-Suite]] pointed at a different job: one named role per stage, each
with a stated deliverable, and a human who only approves.

It is also an advert. The last slide sells the setup, so read the numbers as
claims rather than as findings.

All ten slides are recorded here. Four were unreadable when this note was first
written and Byron supplied them the next day.

## The shape of it

One creator, one office, seven employees, and every one of them says the same
two things about itself: **what it delivers**, and **the three to five steps it
takes to get there**. Nothing in the whole setup describes an employee by what it
is busy with.

`Research` -> `Hook` -> `Script` -> `Design` -> `Publish` -> `Analyze` -> repeat

The line under the loop is the pitch: **your only job is approving the output.**
The human is a gate on the way out, not a worker inside it.

## The seven

### 01 The Researcher, Research dept.
*It finds every viral opportunity.*

Tracks competitors by monitoring top creators and brands. Finds trends, spotting
patterns before they go viral. Saves viral posts as references. Finds new niches,
looking for untapped opportunities. Claims forty sources scanned.

* **Toolkit** Reels, TikTok, Shorts, X, Reddit
* **Delivers** trend reports, content ideas, viral references, opportunity lists
* **Steps** discover, collect, report

### 02 The Hook Writer, Hook dept.
*It owns the first two seconds.*

Writes ten openings for every idea. Leads with proof, on the rule that numbers
beat promises. Kills weak lines, because generic dies in the draft. Matches the
format, tuning hooks per platform.

The funnel is stated as a ratio: **10 written, 3 survive, 1 gets the post.**

* **Delivers** hooks per idea, opening lines, title variants, thumbnail text
* **Steps** draft (ten openings, fast), cut (keep what stops thumbs), hand off
  (winner goes to script)

### 03 The Script Writer, Script dept.
*Every word is planned.*

Structures every script the same way: hook for the first three seconds, value as
the core, proof, then the CTA as the ask.

* **Toolkit** Google Docs for structured scripts, **Claude** to refine every
  word, voice notes to capture ideas, a teleprompter for pacing
* **Delivers** reels scripts, carousel scripts, youtube scripts, captions and
  CTAs, hashtag packs
* **Steps** idea, outline, write, refine, deliver

Worth noticing: the model is a tool *inside* this role, not the role itself. The
employee is the process; Claude is one of its four tools, sitting alongside a
teleprompter.

### 04 The Designer, Design dept.
*It designs every post.*

Works to a fixed brand: ink, blue, an accent, and SF Pro.

* **Toolkit** Figma for layouts, carousels and UI, Canva for quick graphics,
  ChatGPT Images, Photoshop for editing and mockups
* **Delivers** carousel designs, thumbnails that CTR, reel covers, brand assets,
  templates and mockups
* **Steps** brief, concept, design, revise, deliver

### 05 The Analyst, Data dept.
*It tells me what actually worked.*

Finds winners among top performing content. Benchmarks against top creators.
Learns which patterns get views and followers. Turns the data into better
content rather than into a report.

* **Delivers** views and impressions, retention and watch time, shares and saves,
  comments and engagement, follower growth
* **Steps** track, analyze, learn, optimize

### 06 The Manager, Operations.
*It runs the whole company.*

Plans what gets created and when. Assigns work to the other six and keeps it
moving. Connects the systems so handoffs happen unasked. Tracks every employee's
output. Publishes daily.

* **Delivers** everything connected, everything automated, one source of truth

What it delivers is not content at all. It is the only role whose output is the
state of the other six.

### 07 The Publisher, Publishing.
*It posts. On time. Every time.*

Schedules posts at peak times, publishes across every platform, writes captions
designed to perform, never loses momentum, and confirms and logs each post.

* **Toolkit** Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn
* **Delivers** on time every time, reach across platforms, consistent growth,
  zero manual misses, complete reports
* **Steps** plan, schedule, publish, confirm, report

## A contradiction in the source

Slide 9, headed "the loop", lists the seven in employee-number order and so puts
`Analyze` before `Plan` and `Publish`. Slide 7, the Manager's own chip row, runs
`research -> hooks -> scripts -> design -> publish -> analyze -> repeat`.

The Manager's version is the coherent one. You cannot analyse a post you have
not published, so slide 9 is an artefact of listing employees rather than a
sequence anyone could run.

## What is worth taking

**Every role states what it delivers.** Not what it works on, what lands on the
desk at the end. "Trend reports, content ideas, viral references, opportunity
lists" is a contract you can hold a desk to. "Monitors the market" is not. Our
seven desks each have levers and a `learn()` pass, and most can say what they
changed, but not all of them can say what they produced.

**Every role states its method as steps, three to five, always the same ones.**
That is what makes an employee's output judgeable by somebody who did not watch
it work.

**The kill ratio is written down.** The Hook Writer says 10 written, 3 survive, 1
posts. A role that publishes its own attrition rate can be held to it; a role
that says "writes great hooks" cannot. None of our desks states one.

**Every role has a status line.** "Draft 7 of 10." "Queue armed, 6:00pm." "Found
the pattern." "Outline locked, writing." One phrase saying what it is doing right
now, distinct from what it delivers. Our console has this for the bot and not for
the desks.

**One role owns routing and is the single source of truth.** Their Manager is our
[[CEO]] and `comms`. Theirs publishes as well, which ours deliberately does not,
because nothing in our machine may send by itself.

**The stage we do not have is Analyze, as a stage.** In their loop the analyst
sits after publishing and before the manager plans the next round, so the next
batch is chosen by what the last batch did. Our desks learn from outcomes one
number at a time, in [[Memory]] under Lessons, which is the same instinct
arriving one lever at a time rather than as a step in the loop. The weekly pass
added on 15 August 2026 is the closest thing we have to it.
